ℹ️ Info

Map tiles — currently showing

High-res tiles when zoomed

Continent level always uses the bundled NASA texture — nothing streams. Level 5 is bundled too, for both map styles: the complete Esri satellite and OpenStreetMap level-5 tile sets (1,024 tiles each) are built into the game and served only from that local copy — they are never fetched from the tile servers. Zoom in past level 5 and the whole visible view is covered with live tiles streamed from the chosen provider, all the way down to street level: right down to the provider's top tile zoom (19, or 20 on CARTO Voyager), a few hundred metres across the screen at that level. Street names appear from about zoom 16 on the map styles (OpenStreetMap, CARTO Voyager, Esri Streets — all free, no key, max zoom 19–20). Satellite photos carry no names at any zoom, so in Satellite style the game overlays Esri's transparent street & place label tiles on top of the photos from zoom 14. The next zoom level is prefetched in the background and cached for this session, so the next zoom step in is instant. Whenever the tile set changes — zooming to a different level, or switching provider or map style — the new set cross-fades in over the old one instead of popping, and zooming back out to the bundled globe fades the tiles away. While tiles are streaming live, the ℹ️ button in the bottom bar shows a green badge dot. The streaming on/off switch and the provider picker live in the ☰ menu, under Tiles.

Controls

🖐️Drag to spinThe ground tracks your finger 1:1, at any zoom. At wide views (zoom levels 1–5) a flick keeps the globe spinning for a moment after you let go — spin momentum only at those levels; zoom in past level 5 and drags stop exactly where your finger stops
🔍Pinch or scroll to zoomZooming in streams higher-resolution map tiles — a green badge dot appears on the ℹ️ button in the bottom bar while tiles are live
👆Tap the globe to answerIn Find It and Treasure Hunt, tap where you think the answer is
🚶Street View — drag the personDrag the orange pegman in the bottom bar onto any street on the globe to drop into Street View there. While you drag, streets that actually have Street View photos light up in blue on the globe — blue dots at each photo spot, joined into lines along covered streets (live from Panoramax and KartaView, Google Earth style) — so drop the person on a blue spot for a street that is really photographed. Street View shows the nearest REAL street-level photo from the open Panoramax and KartaView collections (free, no key), with the photographer's credit and the date it was taken. True 360° panoramas are preferred — Panoramax panoramas first, then KartaView; flat photos are only a clearly labelled last resort, and where no panorama exists nearby Street View says "No panorama here yet" instead of presenting a flat photo as one. Drag the photo to look around: when another photo is within 50 m, a direction arrow appears inside the view pointing toward that photo, Google Street View style — it moves as you pan and is only there while you are looking toward it; tap it to step to that photo. Photos farther than 50 m get no arrow and no jump. Tap the pegman in the bottom bar (or the ✕) to return to the map. Where nobody has photographed the street yet, Street View says so instead of showing a made-up picture — try dropping the pegman nearer a town. The pegman button only wakes up at zoom 14 and above — below that it stays greyed out. Tapping the pegman without dragging only searches the current view for real street photos and shows a blue marker for each available photo on the globe — it does NOT open Street View. Tap a blue marker on the globe to open that photo, or drag the pegman onto a street to drop in directly. Tap the ✕ on the marker legend to clear the markers. In Street View, drag to look around — panning is fast with a little momentum, so a flick keeps gliding for a moment before settling.
🌌Sun, Moon & starsThe Sun and Moon sit where they really are right now — their positions are worked out live from the standard astronomy formulas, the Moon at its true distance (about 60 Earth radii away) and showing its real phase. Both are drawn 4× bigger than life so you can actually see them. The stars are the real sky too: the 1,627 brightest stars from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue at their true positions, sized by brightness and tinted by their real colour, with the constellation stick figures drawn in — the starfield used to be random dots; now Orion, the Plough and the Southern Cross are genuinely there. Everything wheels slowly overhead as the Earth turns.
